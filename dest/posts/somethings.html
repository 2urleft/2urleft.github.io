lost my ego today because i failed a math entrance exam to 10th grade even when i am

[1] exactly what the title said, i'm now considered to be below average in math as a vietnamese because cambridge sucks. so today i'm going to take notes on 9th grade math that i missed and retake the entrance exam on my school's LMS. also as to why i failed the entrance exam and still go to high school: the transition to upper secondary in my school doesn't require the nationwide entrance exam like normal schools. as you can see, this greatly discouraged me from improving at math.

geometry

[2] in vietnam, you're supposed to learn this in 9th grade, but in my school you're going to learn this in 10th grade. here are some famous identities: $$\sin \alpha = \frac{O}{H}; \cos \alpha = \frac{A}{H}; \tan \alpha = \frac{O}{A}$$ $$\csc \alpha = \frac{1}{\sin \alpha}; \sec \alpha = \frac{1}{\cos \alpha}; \cot \alpha = \frac{1}{\tan \alpha}$$ $$\sin^2 \alpha + \cos^2 \alpha = 1$$ $$1 + \tan^2 \alpha = \frac{1}{\cos^2 \alpha}$$

if $\alpha + \beta = 90^\circ$: $$\sin \alpha = \cos \beta$$ $$\tan \alpha = \frac{1}{\tan \beta}$$

[3] for a triangle: $$h^2=mn$$ $$b^2=an$$ $$c^2=am$$ $$\frac{1}{h^2} = \frac{1}{b^2} + \frac{1}{c^2}$$ a better way to remember is by using a simultaneous called "hệ thức lượng tam giác vuông": $$ \begin{cases} AB^2 = HB.BC \\ AC^2 = HC.BC \end{cases} \implies \frac{HC}{HB} = \frac{AC^2}{AB^2} $$

[4] for an inscribed circle (inside a triangle): $$S = r\frac{a+b+c}{2}$$

algebra

[5] hệ thức viète is a simultaneous for all polynomials that describes the addition and product of roots. the one learnt in 9th grade is for the quadratic: $$ \begin{cases} x_1+x_2=-\frac{b}{a} \\ x_1.x_2 = \frac{c}{a} \end{cases} $$ also as a result: $$x_1^2+x_2^2=(x_1+x_2)^2 - 2 x_1 x_2$$

[6] for "exceptional" students in math, they also learn the AM-GM inequality: $$\frac{x_1+...+x_n}{n} \ge \sqrt[n]{x_1...x_n}$$ and cauchy-schwarz: $$(x_1^2+x_2^2+...)(y_1^2+y_2^2+...)\ge (x_1y_1+x_2y_2+...)^2$$ $$\frac{1}{x_1} + \frac{1}{x_2} + ... \frac{1}{x_n} \ge \frac{n^2}{a_1+a_2+...+a_n}$$

[7] modular arithmetic might also be prominent: $$a \vert b, a \vert c \implies a \vert (b+c)$$ $$\gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, a \mod b)$$
